Tomohiko Mihoya
called me:
“Goodmorning Mr. Kuramata, we finally found it.”

I waited that call since 1964 when I first met Tomohiko at the glass company. I waited 12 years: finally, my project could be realized. Back then there were no glue able to leave no marks on glass, but now everything was different.

Everyone was calling me crazy. A chair made out of glass? Why would one sit on a chair made out of glass, is so dangerous!

They did not understood me.
I want to achieve a, weightlessness, floating, effortless effect.

Took me only 30 minutes to design it (I know I waited 12 YEARS but, ehi, I’m a busy designer). I wanted to make something three dimensional from just a plane sheet of glass.

No more complex japanese joints or mounts or reinforcement or mounts, not even traces.
To the point of disappearence.

You might think I’m mad, but this is what I wanted to achieve. 5 pieces of glass and a glue that polymerize with 10 minutes of sun.
A chair forged by light.

It may be that the chair will abruptly fall in to pieces, which is not a bad thing.

And designers don’t think about this kind of stuff,
insurance company do.

This chair is the correct answer to the type of furniture Kubric should have used for 2001 Space Odissey.

(sorry Sarinen, but i’m just better)

THE END

Short-(comic-fictional)-story of Shiro Kuramata glasschair.
